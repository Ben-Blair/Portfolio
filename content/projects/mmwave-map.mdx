This is the project that convinced me a whole room can live inside a web page.

The scan above is real — my garage, captured and trained into a 3D Gaussian splat, converted to
a 9.8 MB `.splat`, and rendered live in your browser with **Spark.js** on top of Three.js. No video,
no pre-rendered turntable. Every frame is being reconstructed from a few hundred thousand
anisotropic Gaussians as you move.

## Why splats instead of a mesh

Photogrammetry to a textured mesh loses exactly the things that make a space feel like a place:
soft shadows in corners, the fuzz on a rug, the way light falls off a wall. Gaussian splatting keeps
all of that, because it never commits to surfaces at all. The tradeoff is that you're rendering a
point cloud with a million-ish parameters, which is why the browser side needed real attention.

## The orb

The prototype adds a glowing orb you can move through the room, with floor lighting probes sampling
the scene's lightness so the orb's contribution reads as though it belongs there. Splats don't
respond to lights the way a shaded mesh does, so this is a bit of a trick — precompute lightness per
region, then modulate. It holds up better than it has any right to.

## Where the radar comes in

The longer-term idea is fusing the visual scan with **mmWave radar** for presence and motion:
the splat gives you a photoreal static room, and the radar gives you what's moving in it without a
camera. One is a map, the other is a live layer on top of it.
